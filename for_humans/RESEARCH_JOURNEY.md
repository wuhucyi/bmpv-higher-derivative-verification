# Research journey

This document records the chronological development of the calculation. It is
a curated research history, not a verbatim conversation and not evidence that
one prompt produced the result. Candidate formulas were accepted only after
exact symbolic checks. Human researchers fixed the theory, conventions,
boundary conditions, physical interpretation, and standard of proof; AI
assisted with derivation, simplification, code generation, and audit.

## 1. Defining the problem

**Starting point.** The project combined the published static four-derivative
STU result and its equation-solving strategy with the rotating
Gutowski--Reall/BMPV background. The static result supplied regression data;
the BMPV notebooks in this repository provide the executable verification
chain. No external STU notebook collection is required.

The target was the first-order correction generated by a complete
supersymmetric four-derivative invariant. The calculation was restricted to
the stationary equal-angular-momentum BMPV ansatz. From the outset, first
order in the higher-derivative coupling and exactness in the rotation
parameter were separate questions.

## 2. Making the equations explicit

**Method decision.** The first deliverable was an explicit equation archive.
The thirteen first-order equations were organized as

\[
E_{2,\mathrm{linear}}[\delta\Phi]+E_4[\Phi_0]=0,
\]

where the first term contains the unknown correction fields and the second is
evaluated only on the uncorrected BMPV background. The homogeneous operators
were compressed for readability, while the potentially very long source
terms remained explicit.

**Limitation.** The lightweight derivation notebook contains resource-bounded
stages. It is not presented as a cheap complete regeneration of every
curvature and action tensor. The decisive evidence is the archived equations
and later literal substitution into them.

## 3. Establishing benchmarks and the static seed

**Benchmark.** The equal-charge rotating solution was solved first and checked
directly in all thirteen equations.

**Proof.** The generic unequal-charge static correction was then verified
symbolically in all thirteen equations. This supplied the static seed for the
rotating problem and later allowed an exact comparison between the
areal/Schwarzschild-like proof coordinate and the harmonic/isotropic-like
paper coordinate.

**Human correction.** A collaborator identified that the machine solution and
the earlier paper used different radial gauges. The resulting two-way static
radial map was proved exactly. Applying its rotation-independent pullback at
finite rotation defines a useful paper-matched coordinate, but it is not
claimed to be the unique fully isotropic rotating gauge.

## 4. Solving the slow-rotation hierarchy

**Discovery.** The generic unequal-charge system was separated by rotation
parity. The linear-in-rotation sector was solved symbolically and normalized
at fixed physical charges. The parity-even quadratic sector and parity-odd
cubic sector were then treated separately.

**Failed direct integration.** A bounded direct symbolic integration attempt
for the quadratic sector did not produce a useful closed solution.
Intermediate normal forms introduced apparent singular factors, and a
rational ansatz restricted too narrowly to the obvious harmonic-function
poles failed at several tested orders. These failures ruled out the ansatz,
not the existence of a rational solution.

**Underfit collocation.** Exact rational collocation was used for discovery.
Too few sample points produced a spurious one-dimensional nullspace that
failed at unused points. Increasing the number of exact points removed the
false mode.

**Successful discovery route.** Solutions at several exact unequal-charge
triples were reconstructed over the rationals, factored into physical
denominators and charge-symmetric polynomials, lifted back to generic charges,
and substituted into the arbitrary-charge equations.

**Proof.** Collocation itself was never treated as proof. The generic
quadratic formulas gave literal zero in the full symbolic reduced system, and
the cubic formulas gave literal zero in all four symbolic odd equations.

## 5. From a slow-rotation series to exact rotation dependence

**Hypothesis.** The simple finite pattern suggested that the first-order
correction might terminate in the rotation parameter.

**Proof.** A source-degree bound was established. The parity-even correction
terminates at quadratic order, the parity-odd correction at cubic order, and
every allowed higher residual coefficient through the maximum source degree
vanishes. Literal substitution into the archived equations then established
the generic unequal-charge five-dimensional solution as exact in rotation at
first order in the four-derivative coupling.

At linear order in the couplings, the other standard-Weyl STU corrections
follow by cyclic permutation and superposition. This is a symmetry corollary;
the directly verified representative is the single-coupling calculation, and
no quadratic products of couplings are retained.

## 6. Physical and convention audits

**Orientation correction.** The flat-frame convention was fixed to
\(\epsilon_{01234}=+1\) and \(\epsilon^{01234}=-1\). Curved Levi--Civita
tensors retain their vielbein or determinant factors. Executable guards and a
retrospective parity-odd audit showed that the early version was
under-documented but did not require reversal of the certified metric, vector,
charge, or angular-momentum results.

**Gauge-sign correction.** The gauge potentials were aligned with the
positive BMPV convention, preventing an inconsistent sign bridge.

**Physical proof.** Page charges and equal angular momenta retain their adopted
fixed-charge normalization. Horizon regularity holds on the positive-gap
branch. No perturbative claim is made at the classical zero-area endpoint.

**Source check.** Both curvature-free vector invariants, including their
\(F^4\) terms and first variations, vanish on the rotating BPS background.
They therefore do not correct the solution or entropy at first order. This
does not permit removal of the vector completion terms inside the active
supersymmetric curvature-squared invariant.

**Entropy audit.** The Wald--Tachikawa entropy was computed directly and
independently reconstructed. Apparent disagreements with other rotating
entropy formulas were traced to the ensemble and parameter dictionary: fixed
physical Page charges must not be confused with fixed harmonic parameters or
differently normalized spin variables.

## 7. Extending the heterotic-to-STU field map

**Separation of claims.** Verification of the five-dimensional equations of
motion was kept logically independent of the stronger ten-to-five-dimensional
field-map problem.

The rotating reduction, winding/Lorentz--Chern--Simons sector, convention
dictionary, static anchor, lower-two-form direction, and postdual scalar map
were developed in stages.

**Winding-sector correction.** A revised winding potential superseded an
earlier compact expression and an intermediate formula that omitted essential
quadratic and cubic rotation terms.

**Sampled scalar-map trial.** The repaired rotating scalar map first passed
five exact charge/rotation samples. Those samples were discovery tests only.

**Proof.** A later calculation kept all charges, rotation, and radius symbolic
and proved the scalar residual identically zero.

## 8. The third-vector obstruction and the rejected route

**Rejected three-form-first route.** The historical strategy assembled the
corrected three-form first and attempted to infer the final third vector. At
generic rotation the object was nonclosed. A concrete nonzero closure residual
survived, and tied-profile constant-sector repairs were inconsistent. Adding
the scalar repair at the wrong predual stage also failed. This route is
retained as a negative control and is not the physical final definition.

**Successful raw-potential-first route.** The calculation was reorganized
around the raw third-vector potential. Its physical minus-duality relation was
proved for arbitrary radial profiles, and closure supplied an independent
Bianchi guard. A closed solution-space shift, followed by the certified
postdual redefinition and radial pullback, reproduced the five-dimensional
third vector with zero component and Bianchi residuals for generic unequal
charges and arbitrary rotation.

**Claim boundary.** The coefficients of this shift were fixed by comparison
with the independently equation-of-motion-certified five-dimensional target
and then recognized in the source profiles. This establishes an exact identity
on the rotating solution space; it does not by itself derive a unique
universal off-shell field redefinition.

## 9. Testing a source-side derivation

**Rejected source route.** The displayed Appendix-B boundary currents were
tested directly. The only displayed current with explicit three-form
dependence gives a vanishing candidate on the rotating source ansatz. The full
corrected Kaluza--Klein scalar, radial pullback, and displayed postdual vector
direction also fail to determine the required shift.

**Underdetermination result.** Closure, rotation parity, the static anchor, and
asymptotic falloff leave a two-parameter family. They do not select the two
target-matched coefficients uniquely. This is an insufficiency and
nonuniqueness result for the displayed source structures, not a no-go theorem
for every possible covariant field redefinition.

## 10. Independent audits and sealing

**Audit loop.** Material repairs were followed by independent audits. Failed
audits were preserved during development, the artifact was corrected, and the
checks were rerun rather than relabelled.

**Final evidence.** The compact record retains fresh-kernel evaluations,
independent entropy and field-map checks, exact notebook/log identities,
claim-boundary reviews, and repository checksums. Existing PASS labels are
metadata; the decisive evidence remains exact symbolic reconstruction and
literal vanishing residuals.

## Final established scope

The established five-dimensional solution is:

- first order in the four-derivative coupling;
- generic in the three unequal charges;
- exact in the single equal-angular-momentum rotation parameter;
- regular on the positive-gap branch;
- normalized at fixed physical Page charges and equal angular momenta.

The rotating scalar and third-vector maps are exact identities on the
stationary BMPV solution space through first order. The work does not
establish second-order higher-derivative terms, the classical zero-area
endpoint, a generic unequal-two-spin non-BPS solution, a unique rotating
isotropic gauge, or a unique manifestly covariant off-shell heterotic-to-STU
field redefinition on unrelated backgrounds.
